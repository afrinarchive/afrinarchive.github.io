Basic Information about Gundê Mezin - Shirawa area

Source: Tirej Afrin

Etymology: The popular name Gund Mezin means 'the big village' (gund + mezin). The pre-Arabization name Zûq el-Kebîr includes the Aramaic word 'Zûq' meaning watchman, overlook, observatory, or high place; thus the full meaning is 'The Big Observatory' or 'The Big High Place'.

Foundation Date/Period: The current village is approximately 350 years old

Ruins: Xerab Şems

Other Landmarks: Ezhar Efrîn

I. Summary from TirejAfrin Site (English) of Gundê Mezin - Shirawa area

Source: https://www.tirejafrin.com/site/kura%20afrin%20markaz-%20zoq%20kaber.htm

Gund Mezin / Zûq el-Kebîr

The following details appeared in the book Çiyayê Kurmênc (Efrîn): A Geographical Study by Dr. Mihemed Ebdo Elî: Gund Mezin, Zûq el-Kebîr /35km, 554m/:

The popular name means "the big village" (gund + mezin), and it is one of the large villages in Çiyayê Lêlûn. Regarding the name prior to Arabization, Zûq el-Kebîr, Xûrî Bersûm (page 195) states that "Zûq" is an Aramaic word meaning watchman, daring one, overlook, observatory, or high place. The full meaning of the name is "The Big Observatory" or "The Big High Place," where the first word is Syriac and the second is Aramaic.

It is a medium sized village among the villages of Çiyayê Şêrwan and Lêlûn. It was attached to the administrative divisions of the Nibil sub district in 1975. The site contains ruins from the Greek eras. Located 1 km to the west is the magnificent archaeological site of Xerab Şems, which contains two churches, one of them on the hillock. The site also contains a standing facade of an ancient pagan temple. It is a beautiful archaeological and touristic site.

The following appeared in the book Efrîn... Her River and Her Green Hills by the writer Ebdulrehman Mihemed from the village of Qetme:

Zûq el-Kebîr is a village in Çiyayê Lêlûn, administratively following the villages of the central district of the Efrîn area, Heleb governorate. It is a large village located on the slopes of Çiyayê Lêlûn and the eastern Çiyayê Seman chain. It follows the Efrîn central sub district. It is bordered to the north by Kalûte and Burc el-Qas; to the south by a slope, a deep valley, a rugged mountain chain of rocks, the village of Tamûra, and the village of Başemre; to the east by a slope, a rugged mountain chain, and the village of Zehra; and to the west by a mountain chain, the remains of huge Roman church walls at a distance of 30 meters, the village of Kebaşîn, and Burc Heyder.

The number of its houses is approximately 150, and the age of the current village is about 350 years. An electricity network and a primary school are available in the village, as well as a water network from a spring within the village itself which belongs to the state. The old village is connected to the city of Efrîn by a paved road that cuts through the center of the village. There is a mosque in the middle of the village. Its old residences are made of stone with wooden roofs, while the modern ones are made of stone and reinforced concrete.

The habitation of the area is ancient. This is indicated by some ruins, walls, an old church, columns, and rock tombs dating back to the Roman and Byzantine eras. The site of Xerab Şems is located approximately 1 km to the west of Zûq el-Kebîr. The residents of the village work in grain cultivation and the raising of sheep and cows. Currently, it has been annexed to the Nibil sub district of the Ezaz area since the year 1980.

Among the social figures in the village is Mr. Ednan Henan (Rehîm), owner of the Ezhar Efrîn school, who contributed to the educational development of the city of Efrîn through his educational institution (Ezhar Efrîn) and many economic projects that benefit the region.

II. Summary of Gundê Mezin - Shirawa area from Ax û Welat

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JhrDdg5-0N4

The documentary explores Gundê Mezin, which translates to the Big Village. It is located in the Şêrawa district of the Efrîn region on the slopes of Çiyayê Lêlûn. An elder named Apê Hesen explains that the village earned its name because of its expansive territory and the large number of ancient ruins within its borders.

Gundê Mezin was founded around 500 years ago by Êzîdî Kurds. For centuries, the entire population practiced the Êzîdî faith, which some villagers also refer to as the Zeredeştî religion. Over time and particularly due to the assimilation policies and oppression from the Baas regime, many residents converted to the Muslim faith, though some families have preserved their Êzîdî beliefs to this day. There are currently about 260 households in the village. Residents of Gundê Mezin later founded the nearby villages of Bircelqos and Kîlotê.

Historically, the village was administratively tied to Efrîn, but the Baas regime severed this connection and attached it to the Arab towns of Nibl and Ezaz. The residents deeply resented this administrative change, as they felt their true center was always Efrîn. During the Rojava Revolution, the village was returned to the Efrîn administration.

The social structure consists of seven main families: Bûdeq, Hemo, Kuko, Cimo, Ûskê, Hasî, and Elêwî. As a minority community surrounded by Arab settlements, the villagers historically faced raids and hostility from their neighbors in places like Qibtan, Kêrbeşîn, Enedan, Îqda, Hreytan, Heyan, Banûn, Nibl, and Nixêbl.

An elder named Apê Ûs recalls his youth when the Fransa forces occupied Sûriyê. He vividly remembers making long nocturnal journeys on foot and by donkey to sell yogurt in Heleb, resting in Enedan along the way, and seeing foreign tanks at the city gates.

Unlike the fertile plains of Hemqê and Cûmê, the terrain around Gundê Mezin is rocky and mountainous, lacking large olive groves. For generations, the villagers have relied heavily on sheep herding and stone quarrying. The local stone is famous and is cut in village workshops to build houses and palaces. Today, it is exported to international destinations including Qeter, Îmarat, and Ostoralya.

Culturally, the village maintains deep roots in traditional music and foraging. Women gather seasonal wild greens to feed their families. Musicians keep traditional melodies alive, playing wind instruments like the pîk and the zemer, passing down the legacy of historic regional artists like Cemîl Horo, Omerê Cemlo, and Baqî.

The village is also surrounded by profound ancient history. It contains historical ruins locally known as Bajarê Roje, which are referred to as Xarab Şems by the Arab population. These ancient sites are linked to the history of Nabo and date back to the early Christian eras of Maron and Sem'an, demonstrating the deep historical layers present in this rugged region.

II. Summary of Gundê Mezin - Shirawa area from Multi Channel

The documentary provides a succinct but culturally rich overview of Gundî Mazin, a Kurdish village located in the historical Çiyayê Kurmênc region. Sometimes referred to by its Arabic designation Zouq Al Kabir, the village possesses deep historical roots. According to the journalist Beyan Elî, the settlement is older than 350 years. Its naming reflects ancient linguistic influences in the area, specifically tracing back to an Aramean Syriac origin which translates to the high place. This descriptive name aligns perfectly with its geographical positioning.

Geographically, Gundî Mazin is perched at an elevation of 554 meters above sea level. It rests upon the picturesque slopes of the mountain ranges known as Çiyayê Lêlûn and Çiyayê Seman. Administratively, the village is connected to the central subdistrict of the Efrîn region, lying approximately 35 kilometers away from the city center of Efrîn. In earlier times, the village was under the administrative umbrella of the nearby city of Nibul.

The population of Gundî Mazin is culturally and linguistically homogeneous. Every resident speaks the Kurdish language. Socially, the entire community traces its ancestral lineage back to the Kurdish Şêrawa tribe. This strong tribal connection reinforces a shared heritage and social cohesion among the villagers. The settlement itself is modest but growing, comprising approximately 175 homes. The architecture in the village shows a blend of eras, featuring traditional old mud houses built with wooden roofs standing alongside more modern concrete constructions.

The area surrounding and within Gundî Mazin is steeped in antiquity. The village grounds contain remnants of ancient civilizations, including historic walls and rock carved tombs that date back to the Roman and Byzantine periods. The village is also situated near several other prominent archaeological landmarks and neighboring sites. Specifically, it shares borders with the archaeological site of Xerab Şems, as well as the historic locations of Kilotê, Bircê Qas, and Kilbaşîn.

In terms of daily life and economic survival, the people of Gundî Mazin maintain a balance between modern pursuits and traditional agrarian practices. A strong emphasis is placed on education within the community. Simultaneously, the villagers rely heavily on rainfed agriculture, dedicating their terraced lands to the cultivation of olive trees and grapevines. Furthermore, the rearing of livestock remains a vital component of their rural economy. Through the narration of Beyan Elî, the documentary captures a snapshot of a peaceful, historically significant village that has maintained its Kurdish identity, tribal roots, and agricultural lifestyle over the centuries.
