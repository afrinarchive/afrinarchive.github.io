Basic Information about Qestelê Xidiriya

Source: Tirej Afrin

I. Summary from TirejAfrin Site (English) of Qestelê Xidiriya

Source: https://www.tirejafrin.com/site/kura%20afrin%20%20%20bilbile%20-%20Q%20%20Xidiriya.htm

It stated in the book جبل الكرد (عفرين) دراسة جغرافية Çiyayê Kurmênc (Efrîn): A Geographical Study: Qestel Xidiriyanlî, /720m/:

Qestel: An artificial water source, whether it was ancient or modern. And Xidiriya is the name of the old village that was nearby.

The village was established in the sixties of the twentieth century through the migration of the population to it from the neighboring villages. Then it expanded, and small professions appeared in it, such as carpentry and blacksmithing, in addition to shops for selling consumer goods.

It stated in the book عفرين .... نهرها وروابيها الخضراء Efrîn... Her River and Her Green Hills: Qestel Xidiriyanlî: A farm in Çiyayê Kurmênc belonging to the district of Bilbile, area of Efrîn, governorate of Heleb. It is a small farm located in a fertile agricultural plain on both sides of the Riya Bilbile-Reco (Bilbile-Reco public road). Most of its residents are from the village of Qestel Xidiriyanlî and the villages neighboring it. It is about 7km away from the town of Bilbile towards the southwest.

It is bordered to the north by mountainous heights planted with olive trees and the village of Sehriyanlî, Hêyamli, and Baxçe Qonax; to the south by a torrential valley and the village of Qestel Xidiriyanlî; to the west by a wide agricultural plain and the village of Gurzêl and Solaqlî; and to the east by a torrential valley and the town of Bilbile.

The number of its houses reaches about 40 houses, and its age is about 40 years. Its dwellings are of stone and cement, and they are recent. An electricity network and drinking water from artesian wells are available in it, and an asphalt road passes through its center to several neighboring villages. It contains several shops, blacksmithing and machinery repair workshops, and service shops. Most of its residents work in the cultivation of olives and vines alongside raising sheep and goats.

II. Summary of Qestelê Xidiriya from Afrin 366

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J29gc_WBKt0

The documentary explores the village of Xidriya situated in the Bilbilê district of the Efrîn region. The village is positioned roughly four to five kilometers from the center of Bilbilê. According to village elder Ebû Farûq the village is currently home to around one hundred households.

When discussing the history and foundation of Xidriya Ebû Farûq explains that his own ancestors came to the area later while the original settlement was established by other families. The foundational families of the village include the Xabû the Xoce and the Xelîçî families with the latter two being paternal cousins. Over time other families gathered in the area such as the Neqş and Êvlîkolê families. As the village expanded it divided into five distinct sections or associated hamlets which included Xozira and Çoloqa. Historically these parts fell under the same village administration and shared a strong sense of unity during weddings funerals and social matters.

The etymology and official naming of Xidriya reflect the broader political history of the region. Ebû Farûq notes that during the period of Arab rule the state authorities systematically altered Kurdish village names. The village was officially registered as Xidir. He compares this process to the renaming of Kobanê to Eyn el Ereb highlighting how local Kurdish identity was affected by state policies.

In terms of local landmarks and geography the host mentions Qestela Xidriya a notable water source associated with the village. The broader cultural landscape is also evoked through a traditional song performed in the documentary which praises regional landmarks like Çemê Efrînê Çiyayê Hawarê and Çiyayê Lêlûnê.

The residents discuss the agricultural heritage of Xidriya. In the past the lands were filled with vineyards fig trees and pistachio orchards but today olive trees dominate the landscape. The villagers express nostalgia for the older agricultural practices and the sense of community they fostered.

A significant portion of the conversation focuses on the current challenges facing the village. Elders like Ebû Egîd and other men lament the lack of basic infrastructure. The village lacks a local market and a bakery forcing residents to rely on mobile vendors and bread deliveries. There is no state electricity and water must be purchased via expensive tanker deliveries. The lack of employment opportunities leaves the youth with few options.

In response to these hardships the host encourages the villagers to rely on communal solidarity and diaspora support. He shares an inspiring example from Gundê Ehmedî and Qizilbaşan where diaspora members collected funds to pave their village roads. He urges the residents of Xidriya and their relatives abroad to invest in local projects to revitalize their ancestral home.

Foundation/Origin Information of Qestelê Xidiriya

Some of its inhabitants migrated to a new location 1km north of it where water is available, and the new village is called: Qastal Khadryanli.

Source: TirejAfrin Site

Possible Village Name Meaning of Qestelê Xidiriya

Khadryanlu: Name of a clan from the large Kurdish Rashwan tribe, and some say it is from the Milan tribe.
